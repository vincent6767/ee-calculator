How Many CRS Points Does a Spouse or Common-Law Partner Add?

Last updated August 20, 2026

Short answer

An accompanying spouse or common-law partner can add up to 40 CRS points — from their education, language ability, and Canadian work experience — but including them also lowers your own core human capital maximum from 500 to 460. In practice, adding a spouse to your application usually costs more points than it gains unless your spouse's profile is strong.

The three spouse factors

Spouse education0 – 10 points
Spouse language ability0 – 20 points (5 per skill at CLB 9+, 3 at CLB 7–8, 1 at CLB 5–6)
Spouse Canadian work experience0 – 10 points, clamped at 5 years
Maximum spouse block40 points

Spouse education points by credential:

Less than high school0
High school diploma2
One-year credential6
Two-year credential7
Bachelor's degree (3+ years)8
Two or more credentials (one 3+ yrs)9
Master's or professional degree10
Doctoral degree (PhD)10

When the spouse factors actually apply

The spouse block only scores points when all three are true: your spouse or partner exists, is not already a Canadian permanent resident or citizen, and is accompanying you to Canada. If any one of those isn't met, the whole block scores zero — and you're scored on the higher without-spouse core tables instead.

Frequently asked questions

Do I get spouse points if my spouse is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident?

No. The spouse factors only apply when your spouse or partner is not already a Canadian permanent resident or citizen and is accompanying you to Canada — if either condition isn't met, you score using the without-spouse core tables instead, which have a higher maximum.

Is it ever better to apply without including my spouse?

It can work out that way if your own profile is much stronger than theirs — the without-spouse core maximum is 40 points higher (500 vs. 460), and the spouse block only adds up to 40, so a spouse with weaker education, language, or Canadian work experience can lower your total overall. This is a scoring outcome, not eligibility advice.

Does my spouse need to take the same language test as me?

No — your spouse's language points use whichever test you select for their profile, and it's tracked separately from your own first and second language tests.
