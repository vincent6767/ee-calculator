What CRS Score Do You Need for an Express Entry Draw?

Last updated August 20, 2026

Short answer

There is no fixed number. Each round of invitations has its own cutoff, set by how many invitations IRCC issues that round and who else is in the pool that day — and category-based draws (French, healthcare, trades, education, and others) routinely invite candidates with far lower scores than general draws. Check IRCC's official rounds of invitations page for the current cutoff, then focus on what you can control: raising your own score.

How a draw cutoff is actually set

IRCC doesn't set a target score in advance. It decides how many invitations to issue in a round, then invites candidates starting from the highest CRS score in the pool and working down until it reaches that number. The score of the last candidate invited becomes that round's cutoff — which is why it moves from round to round as the pool changes.

General draws vs. category-based draws vs. PNP draws

General draws pull from the entire pool, so everyone competes and cutoffs tend to sit highest there. Category-based draws target candidates who meet a specific criterion — French-language ability, work experience or education in a designated occupation, and similar — and routinely invite at scores well below the general cutoff, because the eligible pool is smaller. A provincial nomination bypasses the cutoff question almost entirely for that candidate, since it adds 600 CRS points on its own — see the provincial nomination guide.

Check the current cutoff

IRCC publishes the score and size of every round on its official rounds of invitations page. That's the only reliable source for a current number — treat any fixed figure you see elsewhere as already out of date.

What actually moves your own score

  • A provincial nomination adds 600 points outright — more than every other additional-points factor combined.
  • Canadian work experience adds up to 80 points directly, plus up to 100 more through skill transferability.
  • French ability at NCLC 7+ adds up to 50 points — see the French bonus guide.
  • An accompanying spouse can add up to 40 points, but lowers your own core maximum from 500 to 460 — see the spouse points guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a single CRS score that guarantees an invitation?

No single score is guaranteed — cutoffs move with every round based on who's in the pool and how many invitations IRCC decides to issue. A provincial nomination is the closest thing to a guarantee, since it adds 600 points on its own.

Are category-based draw cutoffs always lower than general draws?

Usually, yes — because only candidates who meet a specific criterion (a French-language requirement, an eligible occupation, education, or similar) are eligible, the competition is narrower and cutoffs tend to be lower than in general draws, though this isn't guaranteed every round.

Where can I see the current cutoff score?

IRCC publishes every round's results, including the cutoff score and number of invitations issued, on its official rounds of invitations page.
